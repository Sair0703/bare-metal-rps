Two microcontrollers, one fair game

A browser simulation of my bare-metal ATmega328P firmware. Each board picks a move, says it’s ready without revealing it, and only sends the move once the other board has committed. Play both sides, or let Board B play itself. Turn up the line noise to watch the protocol recover from lost and corrupted bytes.

What’s on the wire

Every frame is five bytes: sync A5, type, round number, payload, and a CRC-8 checksum. Packets move in slow motion here; the real link runs at 9600 baud and re-sends every 100 ms.

How a round works

  1. Pick and lock. Press Cycle to choose, then Lock. The board starts sending READY, which carries no move.
  2. Reveal only after both commit. Once a board hears the other is ready, it sends REVEAL with its move. Neither side can peek.
  3. Score and repeat. Each board judges the round itself. First to 3 points wins the match.
  4. Recover from noise. Damaged frames fail the CRC and are dropped, stale ones are ignored by round number, and re-sends fill the gaps.
The game logic, frame format, CRC-8, parser, and state machine here follow the same design as the C++ firmware. Built by Sai Anand.
